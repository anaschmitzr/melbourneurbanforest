👋 Welcome to the dashboard

This dashboard brings together Melbourne's urban forest, 82,000+ trees across 18 neighbourhoods, so you can explore how green the city is, and how fairly that greenery is shared.

Here's what you can do:

🏘️Compare neighbourhoods across canopy coverage, park access, tree diversity, and ecological origin.
📍Explore individual trees: click any of the 82,064 trees for its species, size, and live photo.
💬Ask the data: a built-in tool answers specific questions straight from the dataset.
🧭Add context layers: parks, boundaries, neighbourhood names, plus search and measure tools.

💬 Ask the data

The Ask the Data tool retrieves answers directly from the dashboard's database. It's not a general AI chatbot. It looks things up in the dataset, so it works best with specific, data-shaped questions.

which species are the most common in Carlton?
Top species in Carlton:
1. London Plane: 645 trees
2. English Elm: 296 trees
3. European nettle tree: 182 trees
Tip: name a neighbourhood and what you want to know: species counts, canopy rankings, origin breakdowns. Open-ended or opinion questions won't return results.

💡 The idea behind this dashboard

Trees cool streets, manage stormwater, support biodiversity, and improve the wellbeing of residents. But these benefits flow mostly to those living close to the canopy, making where trees are a question of equity.

This dashboard maps canopy coverage, park access, tree diversity, and ecological origin across 82,000+ trees and 18 Melbourne neighbourhoods. It brings together multiple dimensions of Melbourne's urban forest in one place, building on established international benchmarks and extending them with a graded analysis and an ecological origin layer.
